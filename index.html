FAQ

The questions everyone asks first.

How does Station know how long an assignment will take?+

It starts with an estimate from the assignment itself — type, point value, course, and how much time your classmates' patterns suggest. Then it corrects. Every time you finish or skip a block, Station compares the estimate to what really happened and adjusts. By the third week it knows that your stats problem sets run long and your reading responses don't.

You can also set an estimate yourself, and Station will respect it.

What if I disagree with the plan?+

Then override it. Drag a block, skip it, or mark a task as something you'll handle yourself. Station treats every override as information rather than an error, and replans around your decision.

This is why every block carries a Why this line — you can't sensibly disagree with a schedule that won't tell you its reasoning.

Does Station submit anything to Canvas or email my professors?+

No. Station's Canvas access is read-only. It sees assignments, due dates, and submission status, and it writes nothing back. Submitting your work is still your job — Station just makes sure you have the hours to do it.

My professor moved a deadline. Then what?+

Station notices on the next sync and replans on its own. A deadline that moved closer pulls its sessions forward and pushes lower-priority work back; one that moved out frees the hours for whatever's next most urgent. You get a note explaining what changed and why, not a silently different calendar.

What about work that isn't in Canvas?+

Add it manually — a reading your professor mentioned out loud, a grad school application, a club deadline. Anything you add gets scheduled with the same logic as a Canvas assignment. Recurring work, like a weekly problem set, only has to be added once.

My school doesn't use Canvas. Can I still use it?+

Yes, with manual entry and your calendars, which covers most of the value. Blackboard, Moodle, and Brightspace connectors are in progress — tell us which one you need when you request access and it moves up the list.

What happens to my data?+

It's used to build your schedule and nothing else. Station doesn't sell data, doesn't train public models on your coursework, and doesn't share anything with your school or your professors. Disconnect a source and its data is deleted; delete your account and all of it goes.

Is this just a to-do list with extra steps?+

A to-do list tells you what's due. It'll happily show you four assignments and ten hours of work without ever mentioning that you have six free hours before Sunday. Station's job starts exactly where the list stops: deciding which hours the work goes in, in what order, and telling you when the arithmetic no longer works.
